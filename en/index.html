Digital childhood

What happens to children's attention, to their emotions and to their language when screens arrive early in their lives and stay for a long time. Not to frighten anyone, but to give parents and teachers something they can do on Monday morning.

Why this started

It began in a mother's heart

„Această conferință s-a născut, înainte de orice, în inima mea de mamă. Am trăit personal provocările gestionării timpului la ecrane și am văzut, lucrând cu preșcolari, o realitate care m-a șocat: copii cu propriul telefon, unii cu conturi pe rețele sociale.”

“This conference was born, before anything else, in my mother's heart. I have lived through the challenge of managing screen time myself, and working with preschoolers I saw a reality that shocked me: children with their own phone, some with social media accounts.” Working translation from the Romanian.

prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca — “I am a mother of three and a teacher at CJRAE Bihor”, the county educational resource centre.

Out of that observation came a conference, and out of the conference this initiative. It is not a project against technology. It is a project about which parts of childhood risk being displaced when technology arrives too early and stays too long.

The great rewiring

From a play-based childhood to a phone-based one

Over the past two decades the social life of children and adolescents has moved massively onto smartphones, online games and social networks. Jonathan Haidt calls this transition “the great rewiring of childhood”: the shift from a play-based childhood to a phone-based childhood.

This is not a change of gadgets but a change of environment. Technology is no longer only a tool; it is a factor that shapes the experience of childhood. Which is why building a healthy framework for its use remains the adults' job, not the child's.

Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol (“A generation in danger”), Bucharest, Bookzone — the main source of prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca's paper. Every page reference on this site points to that Romanian edition, which is the one the conference material cites.

Three domains

Where the effect shows

The paper behind this initiative follows three lines of development. Each has signs a parent can notice at home.

Cognitive

Attention, concentration, memory and executive function — planning, self-control, cognitive flexibility. Plus sleep, which underwrites all the rest.

Emotional

Tolerance for frustration, the capacity for pleasure and for empathy, relationships with other children — things learned through play and through time spent face to face.

Language

Vocabulary, expression, comprehension and real turn-taking. Children learn to speak from people, not from screens.
