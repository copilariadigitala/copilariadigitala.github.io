A challenge for parents

Three questions

1. Do I know what my child watches online?

You may well be an involved parent who does know what your children are watching today. Make sure you will know tomorrow as well: trends change at an astonishing speed, and we have to stay informed and connected to our children.

2. What are my child's fondest memories?

The most precious memories are made in the real world, alongside real people. Go home and ask your children what their fondest memories are. The answers will guide you.

3. Do I take on the responsibility of protecting my child online?

The fact that you have talked to your child about the dangers online, and that the child knows the rules, does not replace an adult's guidance.

„Dacă i-ați explica unui copil de 5 ani toate regulile de circulație, l-ați lăsa să conducă singur prin oraș? Desigur că nu. Nu are capacitatea, discernământul sau abilitățile necesare. La fel, nu ne putem aștepta ca un copil mic să navigheze în siguranță în mediul online doar pentru că i-am explicat pericolele.”

“If you explained all the rules of the road to a five-year-old, would you let them drive through the city on their own? Of course not. They have neither the capacity, nor the judgement, nor the skills needed. In the same way, we cannot expect a small child to move safely through the online world simply because we have explained the dangers to them.” Working translation from the Romanian.

From the presentation given at the conference.

What you can do at home

Eight things, all of them from the conference materials

They are not recipes and they are not guarantees. They are the things the paper, the pledge and the presentation propose, gathered in one place.

  • Make screen-free places and moments in the house — places and hours in which the family talks, learns and grows together.
  • Take screens out of the bedroom. When screens are allowed in the bedroom, many children use them late into the night, and sleep suffers. (Haidt, 2024, p. 175)
  • Sit beside your child when they use a screen. Moderate use, interactive and guided by adults, appears less problematic than passive, solitary exposure. (prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca's paper)
  • Talk to them, a great deal. A child's capacity to learn to speak is directly proportional to the time the parent spends talking with them. (Chapman & Pellicane, 2018, p. 17)
  • Make room for free play and for other children. Adolescents who spend their time with groups of young people have better mental health. (Haidt, 2024)
  • Be the example. Use technology responsibly and in balance yourself — a child copies what they see, not what they hear.
  • Set limits early. Facing behavioural problems as early as possible is precisely why consistency counts. (Cloud & Townsend, 2015, pp. 58–59)
  • Stay firm and warm at the same time. You will be swimming against the current — trends, comparisons with other parents, your own children's reproaches. Limits set with love remain the most precious gift you can give a child.

Bibliography

The books the paper rests on

These are the Romanian editions cited in prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca's paper. We give them exactly as they appear there — most of them are translations, and the original English title is not recorded in the material, so we do not invent it. Where English follows a title in quotation marks, it is a literal translation of the Romanian title, not the name of an English edition.

  • Chapman, Gary & Pellicane, Arlene (2018), Creșterea copiilor în era tehnologiei (“Raising children in the age of technology”), Oradea, Casa Cărții.
  • Cloud, Henry & Townsend, John (2015), Limite pentru copii (“Limits for children”), Oradea, Elpis.
  • Dobson, James (2010), Băieții — cum să îi creștem (“Boys — how to raise them”), Oradea, Imago Dei.
  • Dobson, James (2012), Fetele — cum să le creștem (“Girls — how to raise them”), Oradea, Imago Dei.
  • Ducanda, Anne-Lise (2023), Copiii în fața ecranelor. Cum îi protejăm (“Children in front of screens. How we protect them”), Bucharest, Corint.
  • Haidt, Jonathan (2024), O generație în pericol (“A generation in danger”), Bucharest, Bookzone. The main source of the paper.
  • Siegel, Daniel & Payne Bryson, Tina (2017), Creierul copilului tău (“Your child's brain”), Bucharest, For You.
  • UNICEF România — Sănătatea mintală a copiilor și a adolescenților din România (Scurtă radiografie) (“The mental health of children and adolescents in Romania (a short radiography)”), PDF. Consulted on 25.05.2026. In the paper it is the footnote to a single paragraph.
