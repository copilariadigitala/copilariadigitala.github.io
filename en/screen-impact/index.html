Screen impact

What the cited sources say

This page summarises prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca's paper, „Copilăria digitală — impactul tehnologiei asupra dezvoltării cognitive, emoționale și a limbajului”. Every statement sits next to the source the paper names for it. Where the material names no source, it says so.

The paper and every book it cites are in Romanian. Quotations below are given in the original, each followed by a working translation — clearly labelled, because a back-translation is not the author's own wording.

The starting point

The responsibility of adults

Raising a child does not mean only providing comfort or immediate satisfaction. The adult's role is first of all to support the child's physical, cognitive, emotional and spiritual development, and the forming of a balanced, responsible character. That sometimes means firm limits on technology — limits that can cause temporary unhappiness, but that are essential to the child's healthy development.

„Copii au nevoie ca părinții să le fie limite exterioare, limite care să-i corecteze, să-i direcționeze și care să-i supună consecințelor până în momentul în care aceste limite exterioare, devin interioare. Acesta este motivul pentru care coerența privitoare la confruntarea problemelor comportamentale cât mai devreme posibil este atât de importantă. Lucrul acesta te va ajuta să-ți pregătești copilul pentru viața reală în care nu va obține niciodată TOT ceea ce va dori, indiferent cât va încerca.”

“Children need their parents to be external limits for them — limits that correct them, direct them and subject them to consequences, until those external limits become internal ones. This is why consistency in confronting behaviour problems as early as possible is so important. It will help you prepare your child for real life, in which they will never get EVERYTHING they want, however hard they try.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Cloud, H. & Townsend, J. (2015), Limite pentru copii (“Limits for children”), Oradea, Elpis, pp. 58–59.

In the first years of life children do not yet have the judgement needed for informed decisions. Guiding, educating and passing on values therefore remain the adults' task — and that is why the digital age calls for a deliberate effort of awareness, not just a parental-control setting.

The first domain

Cognitive development

Cognitive degradation

A child's brain is built to seek novelty, but at a natural pace — finding a beetle, building a tower, delayed gratification. Short videos offer something else: a constant, rapid flow of dopamine, with loud colours, sounds and a change of shot every two or three seconds, which is to say instant rewards for no effort. The consequence the paper describes is a desensitisation of the dopamine receptors, after which the real world starts to seem boring, because it no longer stimulates the brain enough.

Executive function

Planning and organisation, self-control and cognitive flexibility all suffer. Apathy-abulia sets in — the absence of motivation through dopamine depletion. The brain learns not to put in effort for satisfaction, which undermines the very basis of later motivation and perseverance, and a dependence on immediate rewards takes hold.

Fragmented attention

„Atunci când adolescenții au acces continuu la un smartphone la acea vârstă sensibilă din punct de vedere al dezvoltării lor, capacitatea de concentrare în curs de maturizare le poate fi perturbată.”

“When adolescents have continuous access to a smartphone at that developmentally sensitive age, their maturing capacity for concentration can be disrupted.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol (“A generation in danger”), Bucharest, Bookzone, p. 179.

The ability to focus on a single task and ignore distractions develops intensively in the preschool years. Haidt warns that screens work as a “cognitive prosthesis”: if the screen does the work instead of the child, the neural circuits responsible for concentration stop working.

Sleep

„Copiii și adolescenții au nevoie de o grămadă de somn pentru a favoriza o dezvoltare sănătoasă a creierului, o atenție și o stare de spirit bune în ziua următoare. Totuși când ecranele sunt permise în dormitor, mulți copii le vor folosi până târziu în noapte — în special dacă au un ecran mic care poate fi utilizat sub plapumă. Probabil că declinul somnului asociat cu ecranele este un factor care a contribuit la tsunamiul de boli mintale ale adolescenților din numeroase țări.”

“Children and adolescents need a great deal of sleep to support healthy brain development, good attention and a good mood the next day. Yet when screens are allowed in the bedroom, many children will use them late into the night — especially if they have a small screen that can be used under the duvet. The decline in sleep associated with screens is probably one factor that has contributed to the tsunami of adolescent mental illness in many countries.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol, p. 175. The hypothesis is the author's own and he frames it as one — “probably”.

Addiction

„Asemenea oamenilor cu adicții de heroină și cocaină, cei dependenți de activități digitale au constatat că «nimeni nu îi mai face să se simtă bine» atunci când nu se ocupă de activitatea lor preferată. Utilizatorul trebuie să crească dozajul drogului pentru a recăpăta plăcerea. Din nefericire, când creierul unei persoane dependente se adaptează contracarând efectul drogului, creierul intră într-o stare de deficit atunci când utilizatorul nu consumă drogul. Fără drog, viața obișnuită devine plictisitoare, ba chiar chinuitoare. Nimic nu te mai face să te simți bine, cu excepția drogului. Simptomele de tristețe, anxietate și iritabilitate sunt enumerate drept semne ale sevrajului pentru cei diagnosticați cu tulburare de jocuri pe internet.”

“Like people addicted to heroin and cocaine, those addicted to digital activities have found that ‘nothing makes them feel good any more’ when they are not engaged in their preferred activity. The user has to increase the dose of the drug to recover the pleasure. Unfortunately, when an addicted person's brain adapts by counteracting the effect of the drug, the brain enters a state of deficit whenever the user is not taking it. Without the drug, ordinary life becomes boring, even agonising. Nothing makes you feel good except the drug. Symptoms of sadness, anxiety and irritability are listed as signs of withdrawal for those diagnosed with internet gaming disorder.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol, pp. 187–188.

The second domain

Emotional development

To develop emotionally and socially, children need a great deal of time playing with one another. A phone-based childhood damages precisely the quality of the time they spend together. Children and adolescents who spent a lot of time on social networks are more prone to depression, anxiety and other disorders, while adolescents who spend their time with groups of young people have better mental health.

Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol.

Excessive, early and passive exposure to screens is associated in the paper with:

  • Emotional fragility and low tolerance for frustration. A child who does not learn to handle small frustrations through free play will, as an adolescent, experience any criticism or social setback as a catastrophe.
  • An inability to feel pleasure. A brain accustomed from the age of three to massive doses of dopamine will find real life at fifteen — school, relationships, hobbies — extremely boring.
  • An inability to feel empathy. The neural circuits of empathy do not form in a child raised in front of a screen.
  • Deficits in interpersonal relationships. An adolescent who has missed thousands of hours of face-to-face interaction navigates social nuance with difficulty; errors of interpretation then feed social anxiety.
  • Depression, anxiety, low self-esteem. Girls tend to seek validation exclusively online, which makes them prone to depression through social comparison; boys tend to withdraw from the real world into video games or pornography.

On desensitisation

The quotation that follows describes video-game violence explicitly. If you are reading beside a child, you may want to skip it.

„Copiii de vârste mici învață nu doar cum să ucidă, ci și cum să rămână neafectați atunci când capetele explodează, iar sângele este împroșcat peste tot. Cu puțin exercițiu, ei învață să se obișnuiască cu moartea.”

“Young children learn not only how to kill, but also how to remain unaffected when heads explode and blood is spattered everywhere. With a little practice, they learn to get used to death.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Dobson, J. (2010), Băieții — cum să îi creștem (“Boys — how to raise them”), Oradea, Imago Dei, p. 260.

„Creierul este ca un mușchi: atunci când e folosit, se dezvoltă, devine mai puternic și se comportă mai bine. Iar atunci când este ignorat, nu se dezvoltă optim, pierzându-și o parte din puterea și capacitatea de a funcționa. La asta ne referim atunci când spunem «folosește-l sau îl pierzi».”

“The brain is like a muscle: when it is used, it develops, becomes stronger and performs better. And when it is ignored, it does not develop optimally, losing some of its strength and its capacity to function. That is what we mean when we say ‘use it or lose it’.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Siegel, D. J. & Payne Bryson, T. (2017), Creierul copilului tău (“Your child's brain”), Bucharest, For You, p. 81.

The third domain

Language development

Language is a dynamic, relational process, deeply dependent on the quality of interaction. Children grow up in an ecosystem where face-to-face conversation, books, play, television, the tablet, video calls and educational platforms all coexist from the earliest years.

Which is why “how much time does the child spend on a screen” is not a sufficient question. What also counts is which content they use, with whom, for what purpose — and above all which relational or learning experiences are being displaced.

„Deocamdată nu s-a dovedit că mass-media ar încuraja dezvoltarea abilităților lingvistice la copii. Copiii deprind cel mai bine limbajul atunci când le este prezentat de către o persoană în carne și oase, nu de un ecran. Capacitatea unui copil de a învăța să vorbească este direct proporțională cu durata de timp pe care părintele o petrece vorbind cu el.”

“So far there is no evidence that the media encourage the development of language skills in children. Children learn language best when it is presented to them by a flesh-and-blood person, not by a screen. A child's capacity to learn to speak is directly proportional to the amount of time the parent spends talking with them.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Chapman, G. & Pellicane, A. (2018), Creșterea copiilor în era tehnologiei (“Raising children in the age of technology”), Oradea, Casa Cărții, p. 17.

Neuropsychologically and linguistically, language develops mainly through direct interaction with adults: eye contact, imitation, reciprocal conversation, symbolic and social play. Young children learn language more efficiently from real exchanges than from passive audio-visual stimuli.

The effects associated with screen dependence are a delay in expressive language (vocabulary, sentence construction, morphosyntax, coherence, lexical access), difficulties with comprehension, a poorer vocabulary, difficulties in social communication and in conversational turn-taking, and passive exposure without interaction.

The author's own qualification

The relationship is not simple

This is the one passage in the paper that balances the rest, so we give it whole rather than in a more alarming abridgement:

„Expunerea excesivă, precoce și pasivă la ecrane este asociată cu risc crescut de dificultăți de limbaj, mai ales la copiii mici; efectele sunt mai puternice când ecranele înlocuiesc interacțiunea umană; utilizarea moderată, interactivă și ghidată de adulți pare mai puțin problematică; relația nu este simplă și depinde mult de contextul familial și educațional.”

“Excessive, early and passive exposure to screens is associated with an increased risk of language difficulties, especially in young children; the effects are stronger when screens replace human interaction; moderate, interactive, adult-guided use appears less problematic; the relationship is not simple and depends greatly on the family and educational context.” Working translation from the Romanian.

prof. Ana-Felicia Codoban-Luca, Copilăria digitală — impactul tehnologiei asupra dezvoltării cognitive, emoționale și a limbajului.

In other words: not the screen in itself, but the screen instead of someone. An adult present beside the child changes the equation.

For home

Signs to watch for

This list comes from the conference presentation. It is not a diagnostic instrument, and no single item on it means anything on its own. It is what is worth noticing and, if it accumulates, worth discussing with a specialist.

Cognitive

  • difficulty concentrating and paying attention
  • less patience for activities that require mental effort
  • delayed development of planning and self-control
  • dependence on immediate rewards

Emotional

  • increased irritability and intense reactions to frustration
  • difficulty regulating emotions
  • lower tolerance for boredom
  • sometimes anxiety or restlessness

Language

  • delay in expressive language
  • a smaller vocabulary
  • difficulty holding real conversations
  • passive exposure, without interaction

Behavioural

  • restlessness, hyperactivity or, conversely, apathy
  • increased opposition
  • difficulty following rules
  • preferring the screen to free play or to being with other children
  • possible imitative-aggressive behaviour

The figures

What the data cited by Haidt show

Haidt calls the decline in adolescent mental health that began in the early 2010s “the tsunami of mental illness”. The increases below are the ones presented in the paper, from 2010 to 2024, according to Haidt.

  • +161% / +143%depression, boys / girls
  • +134%anxiety
  • +72%ADHD
  • +91% / +167%self-harm, boys / girls
  • +48% / +188%suicide, boys / girls
  • 1 in 4adolescents say they feel lonely
Haidt, J. (2024), O generație în pericol, Bucharest, Bookzone. The conference material does not state the pages, the country or the reference population for these percentages; we have asked the author and will add them here.

Haidt adds that suicide has become the second leading cause of death among adolescents, and that the rate of alienation at school has also risen.

A deliberate omission

The figures about Romania are missing from this page

The conference material also contains several figures about Romania — a position in a WHO ranking, a percentage of children with withdrawal symptoms, a number of children registered with mental disorders, a percentage rise in neurodevelopmental disorders. You will not find them here.

The reason is simple: for none of them does the material name a study, a year and authors that can be checked. A figure without a source looks exactly like a figure with one, and that is precisely why we do not publish it. The bibliography contains a single online reference — a UNICEF overview of the mental health of children in Romania — but it is the footnote to one paragraph, not the source of the WHO ranking and not the source of the national institute's number.

We are checking them. When we have the title, the year and the authors, the figures will appear here with their citation attached.

What follows

Our shared responsibility

The family

Parents set the rules, keep an eye on the content and create a safe environment at home.

The school

Teachers guide, inform and model pupils' digital behaviour.

Together

We cannot remove technology, but we can learn to manage it and protect children from excessive exposure.

„Trebuie să ne protejăm copiii și să îi învățăm să distingă ce este bine de ce este rău. Permisiunea de a naviga neprotejați și nesupravegheați prin spațiul virtual și prin lumea divertismentului actual este echivalentă cu neglijența părintească.”

“We must protect our children and teach them to tell right from wrong. Permission to roam unprotected and unsupervised through virtual space and through today's entertainment world is the equivalent of parental neglect.” Working translation from the Romanian.

Dobson, J. (2012), Fetele — cum să le creștem (“Girls — how to raise them”), Oradea, Imago Dei, p. 314.
